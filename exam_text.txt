Technical Code Test
Bank Interest-Rate Sensitivity
Overview
This test requires basic knowledge of corporate finance, monetary policy, and banking.
This test assesses core empirical skills, including data preparation and analysis, clear
presentation and summary of results, and the use of economic reasoning to interpret
evidence.
The test has two questions. Question 1 has three parts: describe the provided data, estimate
two interest-rate sensitivities for each bank, and then use the estimated sensitivities in
across-bank regressions and link the banks to stock market data. Question 2 is an open-
ended extension.
Question 1 uses the four provided filescallreports_1984_2017.dta, FedFunds.csv, Bank_
and_CRSP_Linking_Table.csv, andCRSP_Monthly_Stock_File.csv.

Part 2. Bank-Level Interest Income and Expense Betas[20 points]
Economic meaning
For this task, a bank’sinterest income betaandinterest expense betameasure how
sensitive the rates it earns on its assets and pays on its funding are to changes in the federal
funds rate. A larger beta means that the corresponding rate responds more strongly to
changes in short-term interest rates, while a smaller beta means that it responds less.
